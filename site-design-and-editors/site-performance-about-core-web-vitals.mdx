Core Web Vitals are a set of three metrics that assess the experience visitors have when browsing your site. These metrics measure the loading time, interactivity, and visual stability of your site.

### What are the Core Web Vitals?

Core Web Vitals are important as they measure your site's performance with data from real world usage and visitor experience, as well as lab data from simulated sessions. They display how your site is currently performing, and can provide insights on areas that may need improvement.

The three Core Web Vitals currently in use by Google are as follows:

**Largest Contentful Paint (LCP):** An indication of a site's loading time - more specifically, how long it takes for the largest content element in the site's viewport to load.  **Interaction to Next Paint (INP): **An assessment of your site's responsiveness to all click, tap, and keyboard interactions that occur throughout each visit on every page. The final INP value is the longest interaction observed.   **Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS):** A measurement of unexpected movements of elements that change the layout of the page.

These vitals, along with other measurements, accumulate to provide an overall score for your site's pages.

---

### Improving the Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) metric

The Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) measures the time it takes for the browser to load and display the largest element, excluding background images.

---

### Improving the Interaction to Next Paint (INP) metric

Enhancing the Interaction to Next Paint (INP) metric involves simple steps to streamline your site's performance.

#### To improve your site's INP metric:

Review any third-party code snippets added to your site. You can easily review and manage these snippets within the [Custom Code Settings](https://support.wix.com/en/article/embedding-custom-code-on-your-site), [Marketing Integrations](https://support.wix.com/en/article/about-marketing-integrations), and scripts associated with [third-party apps](https://support.wix.com/en/article/accessing-your-apps). Consider removing any code that doesn't provide clear value to your site's functionality.  Limit the number of apps and widgets on each page. Pages overloaded with applications and widgets tend to be more complex and prone to issues. Simplify your pages whenever possible, and refrain from excessive use of applications and widgets that aren't essential to your site's purpose.

---

### Improving the Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) metric

Improving the Cumulative Layout Shift (CLS) metric involves making simple changes to keep your site's layout stable.

#### To improve your site's CLS metric:

Consider reorganizing your site's [DOM order](https://support.wix.com/en/article/site-performance-your-sites-dom-order):

- **Wix Editor: **The DOM order is automatically structured for left-to-right scripts. However, if your site uses a right-to-left script such as Arabic or Hebrew, you'll need to manually adjust the DOM order in the Layers panel.
- **Editor X:** There's no automatic DOM order in Editor X. However, you can [review and rearrange the order of your components](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-reorganizing-elements-by-dom-order) through the Layers panel.

**Note: **Editor X sites are transitioning to Wix Studio, where similar functionalities apply.
- **Studio Editor:** Wix Studio sites have an automatic DOM order, with the option to review and adjust the order of the components via the [Layers panel](https://support.wix.com/en/article/studio-editor-working-with-layers).
    Update ad placements to minimize their impact on CLS. Placing banner ads at the top of the page can disrupt layout stability. Instead, consider positioning ad components below the fold, such as with [Google AdSense ads](https://support.wix.com/en/article/apps-made-by-wix-adding-and-setting-up-the-wix-monetize-with-adsense-app), to reduce CLS issues.

### Performance measuring tools

You can use various tools to gain insights into your site's performance. These tools typically include real visitor data and estimated lab data.

> **Important:**
>
> We highly recommend focusing on real visitor data. You can view this data in two places: the Wix Site Speed dashboard's "Real Visitor Experience" section and Google PageSpeed Insights' "Core Web Vitals Assessment" section. Use these metrics to best understand what your site visitors experience when they load and navigate your site.

#### Click below for more info about performance measurement tools:

### Wix Site Speed dashboard

The [Site Speed dashboard](https://www.wix.com/my-account/site-selector/?title=Select%20a%20Site&buttonText=Site%20Speed%20dashboard&actionUrl=https%3A%2F%2Fmanage.wix.com%2Fdashboard%2F%7B%7BmetaSiteId%7D%7D%2Fanalytics/performance/site-speed) reveals your site's current loading speed and offers actionable tips to enhance page loading.
The dashboard consists of two main sections:
- **Real visitor experience:** This section presents data based on the actual experiences of your site visitors during loading. You can view the average speed of your entire site and a breakdown of each page's average speed. Average scores are derived from data collected over the past 30 days. It can take at least two weeks to see substantial metric improvements.

[image: A Screenshot of the Site Speed dashboard showing the real visitor experience.]

**Note: **Unlike Google PageSpeed Insights, which only includes Chrome browser visits, this data comes from Wix Analytics and includes visits from all browsers.
- **Google PageSpeed simulation:** This estimates how well your site performs using Google Lighthouse. It pretends to load your site on a slow mobile connection. This can help if your site doesn't get enough visitors for the Real Visitor Experience section to show data.

[image: Screenshot showing the Google PageSpeed simulation that can be used if a site doesn't have enough traffic yet.]
Learn more about [using the Site Speed dashboard](https://support.wix.com/en/article/understanding-your-site-speed-dashboard) for tips on improving performance.

### Google PageSpeed Insights

[Google PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) checks how well your site's pages perform on desktop and mobile. It uses the Chrome User Experience (CrUX) report to study real visitor performance. To give your page a Performance Score, this tool uses Google Lighthouse. You can also get constructive suggestions for areas of improvement. 
[image: A Screenshot showing Google PageSpeed Insights.]
Learn more about [improving your site's performance with Google PageSpeed Insights](https://support.wix.com/en/article/site-performance-understanding-pagespeed-insights).

### Core Web Vitals Report

Core Web Vitals are included as a displayed metric in both Google PageSpeed Insights and Lighthouse reports. To view Core Web Vitals as a separate report, you can open them in your Google Search Console. 
**To view a Core Web Vitals report in your Google Search Console: **
1. [Open your Google Search Console](https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/10267942?hl=en).
2. Select a property (domain).
3. Click **Core Web Vitals **under **Experience**.
4. Select either the mobile or desktop report.

**Note: **Google only generates Core Web Vitals reports for sites with sufficient traffic.[image: A Screenshot showing both the Mobile and Desktop Core Web Vitals report.]

### Other third-party testing tools

Wix sites are built differently compared to traditional HTML sites. When using external performance tools, keep these factors in mind:

- Wix cannot guarantee the accuracy of third-party tests. External speed tests use a "black box" approach that only analyzes external features, so results can vary depending on the tool used.
- The amount of content on your site determines the number of certain files (.js, .woff, etc.). Changes you make to your site's content also impacts the number of files.
- Some fixes suggested by third parties may be beyond your control. While certain changes may not be possible on your end, Wix is constantly working to improve performance.

To optimize your site's performance and loading time, we recommend following [our best practices guidelines](https://support.wix.com/en/article/site-performance-best-practices).

### The difference between mobile and desktop scores

When reviewing your site's Core Web Vitals, you'll receive two distinct reports: one for mobile and another for desktop. It is likely that the two overall scores will be different.

### Core Web Vitals and SEO

Core Web Vitals are crucial indicators of your site's performance and are among the various factors considered for your site's SEO ranking.

Meanwhile, the desktop version of the same page might receive a full SEO boost for each metric.

Your Core Web Vitals Assessment will show as ‘Failed’ unless you receive a ‘Good’ score for **all three Core Web Vital metrics**. This does not mean the page hasn't received a boost, as each metric contributes a boost independently.

### Why haven't my site's metrics improved after I made changes?

After making changes to your site, we recommend waiting at least 3 weeks to see meaningful metric improvements. Although, the full effect will not be seen until the collection period has passed and all your page views are on the updated site. 
**Collection periods: **
- **Wix Site Speed dashboard: **30 days
- **Google PageSpeed Insights:** 28 days

If you’re following our [performance best practices](https://support.wix.com/en/article/site-performance-best-practices), but your metrics aren’t improving, please [contact us](https://www.wix.com/contact) for assistance.

### How could my site's performance have gotten worse since INP replaced FID?

Since INP replaced FID, the experience your visitors have while interacting with your site has not changed. The only difference is which metric Google uses to determine your site’s performance. 
With the INP metric’s increased visibility, Wix is striving to optimize your site's performance and develop new ways to improve loading time.
