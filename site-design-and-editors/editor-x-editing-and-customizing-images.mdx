### Adjusting your image sizing

You can adjust the sizing of your image with the Inspection tool. Choose between [fixed or fluid measurements](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-units-of-measure) to suit your design needs. Fixed images stay the same in each viewport, and fluid images are responsive, and adjust to different breakpoints.

#### To adjust the image sizing:

Click the image in your Editor.  Click the **Inspector** panel icon  at the top right.   Choose an option under **Size**:

- **Fixed: **Set the width and height of your element in px.
- **Fluid:** Use relative units of measure** **(**% **or **Auto**) to define your element's width and height.

**Tip:** Select the **Scale proportionally** checkbox to automatically adjust the height according to the width.

> **Note:**
>
> It is not possible to adjust the sizing of an image that's [stretched](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-adding-and-uploading-images-to-your-site#stretching-an-image-to-fill-a-section-container-or-grid-cell) to a grid cell, container or section. The height and width of a stretched image are set to auto, so it resizes automatically, according to the dimensions of the parent element.

### Stretching your image to fill a section, container or grid cell

Using the stretch tool, you can set your image to fill a section, container, or grid cell on your site. Stretching your image means it automatically resizes to the dimensions of the parent element, so you don't need to worry about manually adjusting the sizing.

#### To add and stretch an image:

Click **Add** on the top of the top bar of the Editor.  Click **Media**.  Drag an image on to your section.  Click the Stretch icon  on the top right of the image to fill the whole space.

> **Tip:**
>
> When stretching an image over a section, the background color loads first and then the image. In most cases, the image loads fast enough that you don't see the color at all, but some visitors might see a flash of color. It's therefore important to [set the section's background color](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-changing-the-background-of-a-section#adding-a-color-to-the-section-background), even if it's not always visible.

> **Tip:**
>
> You can [set an image as the section's background](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-changing-the-background-of-a-section#adding-an-image-to-the-section-background) from the Inspector panel, adding color and/or pattern overlays.

### Choosing your image's fit mode

In Editor X, every image has a bounding box that acts like a responsive container. These are the blue edges that you see around your image. You can choose how your image is displayed within the bounding box (e.g. Fill, Fit).

#### To choose your image's fit mode:

Click the image in your Editor.   Click the Settings icon .   Select an option from the **Display mode** drop-down:

### Fill bounding box

The image fills the bounding box so there aren't any empty areas, while still maintaining its original aspect ratio.
If the image aspect ratio doesn't match the bounding box proportions, parts of the image are cropped out in the process. For example, when fitting a rectangular image into a square, the sides are cropped to fit that shape.  
[image: An example of an image that was sit to fill bounding box in Editor X]

### Fit to bounding box

The entire image is shown inside the bounding box. If the image aspect ratio doesn't match the bounding box proportions, the image shrinks so it's fully displayed.
The image is centered inside the box, which may create empty spaces like in the example below:
[image: An example of an image that was sit to fit bounding box in Editor X]

### Keep image ratio

The image width is fixed to the bounding box, while the height adjusts automatically to match the image's original aspect ratio.  
This is a great option for non-symmetrical layouts where you want to show images in different heights, like in the [Repeater](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-using-repeaters) below:   
[image: An example of a Repeater showing images in different heights, as they are set to keep the image ratio.]
**Note:**This option is not available for stretched images. If you want to select this option, unstretch the image first.

> **Working with breakpoints:**
>
> The display mode you choose for your image applies to all breakpoints.

### Cropping your image

With Editor X, you can choose how an image is cropped for each breakpoint. This allows you to further customize the site to suit your specifications, as well as provide an ideal experience for visitors on all devices.

#### To crop an image:

Select the breakpoint you want to customize the image for.  Click the image in your Editor.  Click the Crop icon .  Customize the crop to your needs.  Click **Apply**.

---

### Setting the image focal point

You can choose the image focal point for each breakpoint. This allows you to select which part of the image is visible when it's resized for different breakpoints on different screen sizes.

#### To set the image focal point:

Select the breakpoint you want to customize the image for.  Click the image in your Editor.  Click the Focal Point icon .  Select the area of the image that you want to remain visible when it's resized.

> **Tip:**
>
> Whenever a dominant face is detected, we automatically set it as the focal point of your image, however, you can change or adjust the focal point as needed.

### Choosing the scroll behavior

Select how your image behaves when visitors scroll through your site. You can either choose a scroll effect (Parallax or Reveal) so the image is more dynamic and attention-grabbing, or leave that drop-down blank for a more direct, minimalistic look.

#### To choose a scroll effect for your image:

Click the image in your Editor.  Click the Settings icon .  Click the **Scroll Behavior** drop-down and choose an option:

- **None: **There is no scroll effect on your image.
- **Parallax: **The image moves at a different speed as the other elements, giving a 3D effect.
- **Reveal: **The image is revealed as you scroll down your site.

> **Image zoomed on parallax?**
>
> When choosing the Parallax scroll behavior, it's likely that the image will appear zoomed-in inside the Editor. This change doesn't reflect what visitors see on your live site. To see this effect in action, preview your site.

> **Tip:**
>
> Keep your image visible as visitors scroll up and down the page by [setting its position to Pinned or Sticky](https://support.wix.com/en/article/editor-x-setting-an-elements-position-to-pinned-or-sticky).

### Editing an image with Wix Photo Studio

[Use the Wix Photo Studio](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-media-about-the-wix-photo-studio) to create beautiful images without leaving Editor X. The Photo Studio allows you to customize images that you've already uploaded and saves the newly edited version to your Media Manager.

There are a variety of editing options available, such as filters, rotation, and overlays, so you can create an impressive image to accentuate your site and interest your visitors.

> **Note:**
>
> The Wix Photo Studio makes changes to the image source, whereas changes done on the canvas (to the image design or crop) makes changes per breakpoint.

#### To edit an image:

Click the image in your Editor.  Click the Settings icon .  Click **Adjust**.  (In the Photo Studio) Edit the image using [the available options](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-media-using-the-wix-photo-studio-tools).  Click **Save**.

> **In the Media Manager?**
>
> You can also edit images directly from the Media Manager by clicking **Crop & Edit** under the thumbnail image.

### Adding a watermark for copyright protection

Protect your images by adding watermark text in [the Photo Studio](https://support.wix.com/en/article/wix-media-about-the-wix-photo-studio). The text can be designed and customized to suit your brand, and cannot be removed from the file if downloaded.

#### To add a watermark to an image:

Click the image in your Editor.  Click the Settings icon .  Click **Adjust**.  (In the Photo Studio) Click **Text** under **Add An Element**.  Browse the text styles and click to add one to your image.  Type your watermark message.  (Optional) Click **Design** to customize the text.  Click **Save**.
